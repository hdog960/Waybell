# WAYBELL — Creative Design Bible
Version: Phase 0 / 1.0 | Authority: tone, emotional arc, game experience, high-level scope.

## Identity and promise
**WAYBELL: The Roads We Leave.** Original 2.5D fantasy action-adventure. Travel is personal: paths physically connect communities; memories of travel remain in magical Waylines; preserving a world does not mean keeping it motionless.

**Player promise:** A peaceful home opens into a wondrous, interconnected kingdom; each region introduces distinct beauty, people, mysteries, tools, and unforgettable authored dungeons; returning home after the journey feels different. Story target 18–25 hours for a typical main-story playthrough plus substantive optional content, pending actual playtests. Single-player and accessible without grinding.

## Emotional cadence
- Opening: safe, tactile, playful. Thimblebrook's daily life; Nella and Orin establish attachments before danger.
- First reveal: leaving the starting hill exposes The Long Reach and distant visible landmarks while music opens outward.
- Act I: delight, mystery, item-driven discovery. Nella recovered in Brasshollow, not withheld as a finale hostage.
- Act II: discovery of the network's failures, communities displaced and altered; home changes; moral ambiguity of Seren.
- Act III: connection through change, not perfect restoration. The player coordinates a decentralized Wayline network.
- Finale: a complete map gives way to a blank page and an unknown road beyond it.

## Six design principles from classic adventure storytelling (NOT copying Zelda)
- A small familiar starting space makes the larger world meaningful.
- Routes and landmarks act as memory, not a waypoint checklist.
- Dungeon tools gain additional traversal/combat/puzzle meaning outside their origin.
- A few strong character motifs beat generic fantasy background noise.
- Meaningful temporal return: town changes, new arrivals, old companions leave.
- Challenges reward learning rather than high numeric character levels.

## Non-goals and prohibited shortcuts
No endless empty open world, massive survival crafting, random loot rarity economy, live-service, forced ads, PvP, large recruitable parties, sandbox building, procedural story chapters, repeating recolor bosses, excessively long travel, multiple currencies for padding, arbitrary stamina gates, or copied Nintendo IP.

## Campaign structure
- Seven regions: The Long Reach (central hub with Thimblebrook), Wilderbloom (forest), Brasshollow (bell city), Tidemarrow (wetlands), Ashglass Highlands (volcanic forge), Snowglass Peaks (mountain observatory), Broken Horizon (final fractured realm).
- Six major dungeons: Rootbound Mill, Bellfound Choir, Drowned Archive, Hearth Forge, Sleeping Orrery, Unmade Road.
- Three optional: Forgotten Tollhouse, Hollowglass Theater, Reedcrypt.
- Five major tools: Rootspool, Sunlens, Tideweights, Ironhand, Galecloak.
- Echo Lantern: Reveal, Replay, Restore; limited recorded actions and bounded interaction surfaces.
- Approx. 18 substantial optional quests and eight authored Waymaking restoration projects.
- Approx. 18–24 unique enemy behavior families, with meaningful authored variants; fixed 5 major-boss encounters plus finale and 3 optional bosses.
- Main cast: Tavi, Nella, Orin, Seren. The Ferryman a mystery, not a shock betrayal.

## The exploration rhythm
1. Spot a distinctive distant place or strange feature.
2. Walk toward it, remember obstacles and social context.
3. Uncover a self-contained puzzle, NPC story, combat teaching, or route mystery.
4. Gain a tool, insight or relationship change.
5. Recognize that the new insight changes older spaces.
6. Choose whether to follow the core story or explore an optional branch.

## Opening 60-minute vertical-slice intention
0–10 min: village routines and movement, Orin and Nella; 10–20 min: nearby ruin, first small puzzle and combat; 20–30 min: Wayline collapse and Nella's disappearance; 30–40 min: discover Echo Lantern and teach Reveal/Replay in sanctuary; 40–60 min: climb and exit village to cinematic-but-interactive Long Reach reveal. Phase 3 content target includes a small mini-dungeon and combat loop; actual duration needs playtest validation.

## Player fantasy
Be a thoughtful young adventurer who grows more capable through mastery of place and equipment, not dominance through leveling. A winning puzzle solution feels earned, the combat pace supports exploration, and completing side stories changes real spaces.

## Quality bar
An area must be appealing to inhabit before more area is built. Avoid cursor-like characters, distorted sprites, odd feet/shadows, bad depth sorting, obstructed controls, muddy silhouettes, placeholder finales, jump scares in a cozy region, and vague black-box puzzles. Every camera perspective must be reviewed on iPhone.

## Canonical non-canon boundary
Mood images or reference searches may be used to discuss look; they are not concept approvals or game assets. Placeholder dimensions, scores, schedules, musical cues and all time estimates are design assumptions pending test. Refer to docs/02–08 for production specifications and gates.
