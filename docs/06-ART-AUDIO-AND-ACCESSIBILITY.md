# WAYBELL — Art, Animation, Cinematography, Music, and Accessibility
Version: Phase 0 / 1.0 | Authority: aesthetic standards and audiovisual requirements. THIS IS PLANNING ONLY.

## Visual promise
Premium illustrated 3D miniature diorama viewed in three-quarter perspective, using real meshes/volume/elevation, readable animated stylized hero, warm lighting, local ambient motion and recognizable silhouettes. Do not use flat stacked sprites to pretend true depth. Not retro pixel art and not hyper-realism. Warm whimsy early, nuanced melancholy as plot progresses, brighter reunion near ending.

## Reference composition rules
- Every major area has foreground, playable midground, scenic background and an intelligible destination landmark.
- Architecture expresses region culture through materials and proportions; avoid same room reskinned seven times.
- Render depth physically: shadows anchored to feet, platforms visibly connect in z, foreground visibility managed by transparent fade or reposition rather than walk-through walls.
- Ensure readable contrast between usable props and decoration. Reserve strongest saturation for player and interaction cues.
- Use textured but restrained surfaces; no image-generation artifacts baked into textures, glitchy tiling, broken UVs, impossible stairs or clipping figures.
- Avoid visual clutter near mobile HUD; cinematic views must remain clear in landscape safe area.

## Region palette and lighting
Long Reach — honey gold, leaf green, clay red; large sky, warm home interiors.
Wilderbloom — sage/lime, forest umber, moss; dappled light; water reflections.
Brasshollow — brushed brass, charcoal, stained-glass blue; warm night windows.
Tidemarrow — turquoise, muted plum, gray reed; haze and reflective pools.
Ashglass — basalt charcoal, ember orange, rusty red; visible heat shimmer with reduced-effect option.
Snowglass — glacial blue/pearl, indigo sky, understated gold light.
Broken Horizon — washed parchment, violet shadows and disconnected familiar hues; mysterious but not low-contrast.
All colors subject to original visual-concept approval; palette is a starting vocabulary.

## Hero animation standard
Tavi needs distinctive silhouette, consistent proportions, correctly oriented face/hair/lantern across locomotion; 8-direction movement and smooth transitions preferred where practical. States: idle and contextual idle, walk, sprint, stop/turn, melee combo, shield, dodge, stagger/hurt, contextual ledge climb, swim, 5 tool-use poses, Lantern Reveal/Replay/Restore, NPC conversation, item acquire, emotional scripted moments. Character feet contact reliably; no hover, wrong-facing movement or shadow offset. Prioritize cohesive rig, not more animation clips with poor transitions.

## NPC animation economy
Major cast: custom expressive gestures, listening/response states, walk/idle and key story acting. Minor inhabitants use compatible modular animation assets; still need varied silhouettes, attire and credible pathing. Never use a hero duplicate as generic NPC or single static cutout pretending to walk.

## Camera language
Normal: stable three-quarter view, authored perspective distance appropriate for iPhone landscape. Adjust to player elevation and nearby obstructions. Combat: gently frame both target and player; protect telegraph floor. Scenic reveal: deliberate widening/pan, preserving player agency. Interiors: occluding walls fade or hide without revealing through-geometry glitches. No dizzying rotation in traversal; reduced camera motion option. Actual final numeric FOV / zoom decided by prototype and user review.

## Environment visual checks
For each region examine 360-degree traversal, two elevation tiers, behind large props, stairs under/over walkways, transitions, entry after teleport/save, dusk/night, weather, low/medium visual detail modes, and all supported aspect ratios. Hard reject: pop-in revealing void, player behind invisible cover, sprites showing through walls, broken shadows, unreadable interactables.

## UI art rules
Decorative fantasy material framing may be used in menus but must not become visual noise. HUD minimal; icons large and distinct with text alternatives. Control rims look like part of game's world; safe-area and touch placement adjustable. Dialogue panel never covers face of speaking character or forced puzzle view. Roadbook conveys map, journal and objective distinction without clutter.

## Music story identity
Main leitmotifs: Home/Thimblebrook (belonging), Traveler (curiosity), Unfinished (loss and ambiguity), Waybell (hope/connection). Original acoustic ensemble—woodwinds, strings, bells, occasional restrained brass/percussion. Strong region-specific melodic identities. Each Great Bell has a distinct pitched motif that can be layered in finale; avoid deriving copyrighted Zelda cues. Approx. 25–35 original cue/arrangement targets including alternates; subject to budget. Ambient quiet and diegetic world sound equally vital.
- Thimblebrook opening: small intimate Home motif; final return: expanded orchestration with regional notes.
- Long Reach first reveal: controlled crescendo of Traveler motif, not constant epic music.
- Forest: airy woodwinds and soft percussion; Brass: harmonic bell textures and crisp rhythmic detail.
- Marsh: muted strings and spacious water sound; Forge: low metallic pulse; Snow: fragile crystal tones and wind.
- Seren: Unfinished motif with clear hints of compassion.
- Final decentralized activation: motifs converge, no copyrighted melodies.

## Audio behavior and cues
Layered ambient loops, directional water/wind, weather, footfalls by surface, equipment sounds, clear attack audio, UI confirmation. Combat warning is never audio-only; subtitles/captions describe meaningful sounds. Music adapts smoothly to combat and restore events and can be reduced or muted independently.

## Acceptance standards for visuals/audio
- Immediate distinction of all regions from one screenshot.
- Tavi consistently reads as same character from all movement directions.
- No clipping, visible world holes, floating shadows, noisy unreadable depth cues, or decorations intercepting input.
- Interactive props stand out with animation and shape as well as color.
- Musical motifs support important emotional turns and loop cleanly; transitions never pop/crackle.
- Performance tradeoffs preserve player/landmark silhouette and control clarity.
- Owner inspects visual packet and signs off before Phase 2 work.

## Inclusive access
Reduced effects, high contrast markers, color-independent icon shapes, adjustable text/subtitles, audio cues captioned, no required sound-perfect timing, camera comfort options, hint layers, touch layout adaptation and alternative aim settings. Do not make accessibility an optional polish item deferred beyond playable testing.

## Asset provenance and licensing
All final characters/world/audio must be original or transparently licensed for proposed use; record license terms, author/source, permitted redistribution and modifications in a future asset manifest. Never copy recognizable copyrighted motifs, models, textures, art or soundtracks from Ocarina of Time or other games. Concept search imagery is illustration only and NOT permission to ingest as production assets.
